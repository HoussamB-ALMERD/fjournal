# Why Fjournal exists

## The system we are replacing

Science is done by researchers, usually funded by the public. It is reviewed for free by other
researchers. Then a publisher takes ownership of the result and sells it back to the same
universities that paid for the research, or charges the authors to make it readable.

- **The money flows the wrong way.** Open-access fees at prestige journals can exceed
  US$10,000 per article. The largest publishers report operating margins of around 35–40%,
  higher than most technology companies. The scientists who do the work, often on low
  salaries and short contracts, get nothing for it.
- **The labour is free.** Reviewers gave an estimated 100+ million hours to peer review in 2020
  alone (Aczel, Szaszi & Holcombe, 2021) and were not paid for it.
- **Careers are held hostage.** Hiring, promotion and funding depend on *where* you publish,
  not *what* you found. That gives a handful of brands power over every researcher's
  livelihood, and they set the price.
- **The story wins over the truth.** Journals sell novelty and narrative. Results that are
  surprising, clean and on-trend get published. Negative results, replications and inconvenient
  findings get rejected as "not a good fit". This bias is well documented: a large share of
  published findings fail to replicate (in one landmark effort, only about 36% of 100 psychology
  studies reproduced with significant results; Open Science Collaboration, 2015).
- **The gate doesn't even keep out fraud.** Three or four reviewers who never see the data
  cannot catch fabricated data. Top journals have retracted high-profile papers built on data
  that may never have existed, and industrial "paper mills" have pushed many thousands of fake
  papers through peer review. The gatekeeping costs science dearly and still fails at the one
  job it claims to do.

When a few people's preferences decide what counts as knowledge, science bends toward what
those people expect to see. Work that confirms the expected story is waved through; work that
challenges it has to fight. That is not how truth should be decided.

## What we believe

1. **Knowledge belongs to everyone.** Reading science must be free. Publishing science must
   be free. Forever. No exceptions, no "premium tier".
2. **Evidence outranks authority.** A claim is as strong as its data, methods and code, and
   as strong as its record of surviving scrutiny. It does not matter who made it, what degree
   they hold, or where they work.
3. **Evaluation must happen in the open.** Secret verdicts from anonymous gatekeepers are
   replaced by public, signed, permanent reviews that anyone can read, answer and judge.
4. **Every honest result is a contribution.** "It didn't work" and "we couldn't reproduce it"
   are knowledge. They count as much as discoveries, sometimes more.
5. **Authors own their work.** Copyright stays with the people who did the research.
6. **The record is permanent.** Nothing is quietly rewritten. Every version, every review and
   every correction stays visible, with a public, tamper-evident proof of who said what, when.
7. **We grow together.** Review is collaboration, not combat. The goal is not to reject work
   but to make it better and find out what is true.

## We must not become what we replace

Giving power to "the community" does not automatically produce truth. A crowd can have its own
fashions, its own orthodoxies and its own mobs. If Fjournal swaps the journals' agenda for a
majority's agenda, we have failed. So we hold ourselves to these rules:

- **Fjournal has no editorial line.** We never decide which conclusions are acceptable. We
  have no "scope", no "fit" and no preferred outcome.
- **Disagreeing with the consensus is never a reason for removal.** Work can be marked
  *Contested* by its evidence. It is never hidden for being unpopular. Content is removed only
  for fraud, plagiarism or abuse, and every removal is logged publicly with its reason.
- **Reputation rewards useful critique, not agreement.** Reviewers earn standing when others
  find their engagement helpful, whether they praise the work or tear it apart.
- **Data, not trust.** We push every claim toward open data and code, because the only real
  defence against fake results is letting everyone check them. Unchecked claims should look
  unchecked.
- **Every rule is public.** Badges, reputation and moderation all follow published rules
  anyone can audit and challenge. A power that can't be inspected is the power we are trying
  to abolish.

## What winning looks like

We win when a hiring committee, a funder or a journalist looks at a Fjournal record (open data,
open code, a public review history, independent replications) and trusts it **more** than a
journal logo. At that point the journals' monopoly ends. Nobody has to ban them; scientists
simply stop needing them.

Science should belong to the people who do it and to everyone it serves.
